IPA delivery playbook · the short version

How we deliver automation

Every request goes through the same five steps, whether it is Intelligent Automation (RPA) or Smart Automation (Power Platform). Each step has one owner and ends at a CoE gate. The route changes how heavy the gate is. It never changes the steps.

The five steps

Work moves left to right. It only moves on when the step is done and the gate is passed.

  1. STEP 1

    Intake

    Owner: Product Owner

    The request is logged. The Engineering Lead decides the route (IA or SA), a named Ops contact is agreed, and the work gets a first size and risk flag.

    Done when it is logged, routed, sized and has an Ops contact.

    No gate. Nothing starts without it.
  2. STEP 2

    Assess

    Owner: Engineering Lead

    We confirm the business outcome, requirements, data, systems and platform fit. The squad breaks the work down and confirms the size.

    Done when the CoE approves the case (IA) or the register entry is complete (SA).

    SG1 · Opportunity Assessment
  3. STEP 3

    Build

    Owner: Engineering Lead

    The business confirms requirements and grants access. The squad builds the solution, the charter, the route-to-live material and the test plan.

    Done when the build is complete and the test plan is ready.

    SG2 · Build readiness
  4. STEP 4

    Test

    Owner: Engineering Lead

    The squad runs the tests, evidences the results and fixes defects. The business runs UAT and signs it off.

    Done when results are evidenced and UAT is signed off.

    SG3 · Test to Live
  5. STEP 5

    Go live

    Owner: Engineering Lead

    Each scenario is checked by a named business validator the first time it runs live (FOV). Then hypercare, handover to Ops and benefit tracking.

    Done when every scenario is validated, hypercare is complete and Ops own it.

    SG4 · Hypercare to BAU

IA runs the full gate at each step. SA runs the same steps with a lighter check: a register entry instead of a full opportunity case, and proportionate evidence at each gate.

After go-live

Two things happen to a live solution. Both have an owner and a sign-off.

Changing a live solution

Owner: Product OwnerGate: SG5 · Enhancement to Live (major only)

The Product Owner classifies the change by size and risk together, then it takes one of two paths.

  • Minor changeSkips the assessment. Light build and test, then go live with checks and hypercare scaled to the size.
  • Major changeGoes back through Assess and every gate, with full FOV and hypercare.

A small change to something critical still takes the long route.

Retiring a solution

Owner: Engineering LeadGate: CoE decommission sign-off

When a solution is obsolete or replaced, the business and Product Owner confirm it is no longer needed. The squad removes the assets, revokes access and archives the documentation, and the estate register is updated.

Retirement is a planned step on both routes, so the estate stays clean.

Who does what

Business
Raises the need, confirms the outcome, signs off UAT and validates the first live runs. Owns and operates the solution once it is live.
Product Owner
Owns demand, value and priority. Accountable for intake and for classifying changes. Runs the Teams channel.
Engineering Lead
Accountable for delivery from assessment to retirement. Decides the route and submits every gate to the CoE.
Engineers
Break down, build, test and support the work. Responsible wherever the work is technical.
CoE
Approves the gates. Consulted at every gate, never accountable for delivery.

One accountable owner per step. Never two, never none.

Size sets the support

Every piece of work gets a T-shirt size. The size tells you how much build to expect, how many scenarios the business will check live, and how long hypercare runs.

The size is set at intake and confirmed at SG1. Hypercare is a fixed window. First-run checks (FOV) are not: they finish when every scenario has been checked and reconciled, however long that takes. A scenario that fails goes back to build.

Six rules we don't bend

  1. All work comes in through intake. No side doors.
  2. No build starts until the assessment is approved and the business outcome is confirmed.
  3. Nothing reaches production without evidenced testing and CoE approval.
  4. Nothing scales until every scenario has passed its first live run.
  5. Every change to a live solution is classified by size and risk before it moves.
  6. The business accepts ownership before hypercare closes.
