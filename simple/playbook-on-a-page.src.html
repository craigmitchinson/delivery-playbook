IPA delivery playbook · the short version

How we deliver automation

Every request goes through the same five steps, whether it is Intelligent Automation (RPA) or Smart Automation (Power Platform). Each step has one owner and ends at a CoE gate. The route changes how heavy the gate is. It never changes the steps.

The whole journey

Left to right for a new solution. A live solution loops back in for a change, or exits through retirement.

CoE gate. The Engineering Lead submits, the CoE decides.IA runs the full gate at every step.SA runs the same steps with a lighter check: a register entry instead of a full opportunity case, and proportionate evidence at each gate.

Who does what, and by when

Each step in order. Timings are the standard we hold ourselves to, in working days. Anything that misses one is raised at the next daily sync.

    Intake

    Owner: Product Owner

    Done when the request is logged, routed, sized and has a named Ops contact.

    No gate. Nothing starts without this step.
    1. Business raises the demand, with its context, and names an Ops contact.Same day
    2. Product Owner logs it, confirms the outcome and priority, and passes it for routing.Within 1 day
    3. Engineers give current platform capability and squad capacity.Before the route is set
    4. Engineering Lead decides IA or SA on technical grounds, sets the provisional size and the risk flag, and tells the PO and the business.Within 2 days
    5. Product Owner adds it to the pipeline and opens the Teams channel with the pinned post.Next daily sync

    Assess

    Owner: Engineering Lead

    Done when the CoE approves the case (IA) or the register entry is complete (SA), and the size is confirmed.

    SG1 · Opportunity Assessment

    Test

    Owner: Engineering Lead

    Done when every test case has evidenced results, defects are triaged and the business has signed off UAT.

    SG3 · Test to Live

    2. Engineers raise, triage and prioritise each defect. P1 and P2 go to leads with the date impact.Same day it is found

    Go live

    Owner: Engineering Lead

    Done when every scenario has passed its first live run, hypercare has run its window with no P1 or P2 open, Ops are trained and own it, and benefit tracking is live.

    SG4 · Hypercare to BAU

After go-live

A live solution is only touched through one of these two paths.

6 · Change to a live solution

Owner: Product OwnerGate: SG5 · Enhancement to Live, major only
  1. Business raises the change, with the driver behind it.Same day
  2. Engineering Lead gives the technical impact, size advice and risk flag, with squad input on effort.Within 2 days
  3. Product Owner classifies it by size and risk, names the path, and the step it re-enters at: minor goes to Build, major goes to Assess.Within 3 days of the request
  4. Engineering Lead submits SG5 for a major change, or a CoE consult for any risk-flagged change, whatever its size.Before the change moves

A small change to something critical still takes the long route.

7 · Retire a solution

Owner: Engineering LeadGate: CoE decommission sign-off
  1. Business confirms the solution is no longer needed, and why.Same day it is flagged
  2. Product Owner issues the decommission instruction and the agreed removal date.Within 5 days
  3. Engineers remove the assets, disable schedules, revoke access and archive the documentation.On the removal date
  4. Engineering Lead submits the sign-off and updates the estate register.At CoE sign-off

Retirement is a planned step on both routes, so the estate stays clean.

Who owns what

Business
Raises the need, confirms the outcome, grants access, signs off UAT and validates the first live runs. Owns and operates the solution once it is live.
Product Owner
Owns demand, value and priority. Accountable for intake and for classifying changes. Owns the Teams channel and every message to the business.
Engineering Lead
Accountable for delivery from assessment to retirement. Decides the route and submits every gate to the CoE.
Engineers
Assess, break down, build, test and support the work. Responsible wherever the work is technical.
CoE
Decides the gates. Consulted at every gate, never accountable for delivery.

One accountable owner per step. Never two, never none.

Size sets the support

Every piece of work gets a T-shirt size at intake, confirmed at SG1. The size tells you how much build to expect, how many scenarios the business checks live, and how long hypercare runs.

Hypercare is a fixed window. First-run checks (FOV) are not: they finish when every scenario has been checked and reconciled, however long that takes. Nothing scales beyond the checked scenarios until then.

Six rules we don't bend

  1. All work comes in through intake. No side doors.
  2. No build starts until the assessment is approved and the business outcome is confirmed.
  3. Nothing reaches production without evidenced testing and CoE approval.
  4. Nothing scales until every scenario has passed its first live run.
  5. Every change to a live solution is classified by size and risk before it moves.
  6. The business accepts ownership before hypercare closes.

Where things get said

Each piece of work gets a Teams channel at intake. The Product Owner owns the pinned post and updates it at every gate. There are three meetings and no side channels.

Daily sync · Engineers, EL, PO
Progress, blockers, defects, first-run coverage and hypercare status.
Weekly feature sync · POs and ELs, cross-squad
Gate status and any slippage, called out in the week it appears.
Planning & prioritisation · leads and leadership
Portfolio priority, escalations, benefit and capacity.
Escalation A missed timing goes to the daily sync. Slippage goes to the feature sync. A failed gate, or a P1 or P2 defect, goes to leads at planning and prioritisation, with the date impact.
